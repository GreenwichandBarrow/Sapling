# Deal Intake and Three-Pillar Scorecard

Use this reference for CIMs, teasers, decks, broker packages, financial packages, and emails containing deal information. This is part of the canonical `deal-evaluation` skill, not a parallel workflow.

Source frame: Kay provided the Acquiring Minds webinar structure "Score It or Skip It: A Framework for Fast Deal Evaluation" with three pillars: Target Quality, Deal Structure, and Buyer Fit. Also incorporate the June 18, 2026 Acquiring Minds QoE note in `brain/calls/2026-06-18-acquiring-minds-qoe-webinar.md`: revenue quality, customer concentration, EBITDA-to-cash conversion, capex, working capital, add-backs, and wage normalization are first-pass diligence issues.

## Intake Output

Every first-pass deal review must produce:

1. Company basics: name, website, headquarters, sector, industry, founded year, employees, owner/seller names, source, intermediary, process deadline, and document list.
2. Plain-English business summary: what the company does, who pays it, why customers need it, and how revenue repeats.
3. Deal context: source channel, seller motivation, ownership/transition situation, process status, requested action, and known deadline.
4. Financial metrics: revenue, EBITDA/SDE, margins, growth trend, LTM period, add-backs, working capital, capex, asking price, valuation multiple, and proposed structure.
5. Known vs unknown: separate facts, assumptions, and unknowns. Never fill unknown diligence gaps with optimistic assumptions.
6. Three-pillar scorecard: Target Quality, Deal Structure, Buyer Fit.
7. Missing diligence items: concise list of information needed to make the recommendation more reliable.
8. Broker/seller questions: short draft questions when the recommendation is proceed with more information or insufficient information.
9. Recommendation: one of Proceed, Proceed but request more information, Relationship-only, Pass, or Insufficient information.
10. Why this matters for Kay: the practical implication in one short section, focused on time, fit, downside risk, and next action.

## G&B Default Lens

Prefer boring B2B/service businesses with recurring or reoccurring revenue, critical service value, durable customer retention, fragmented markets, and low customer concentration.

Hard-no categories: retail and restaurants, unless Kay explicitly overrides.

Size lens: $3M+ EBITDA is preferred. $750K-$3M EBITDA is reviewable only when revenue quality, customer durability, service criticality, and thesis fit are unusually strong. Below $750K EBITDA is below the current lower bound.

Industry fit positives: compliance-driven services, route-based or repeat field services, insurance and insurance-adjacent services, infrastructure services, outsourced business services, mission-critical vertical SaaS, and services with low AI-disruption risk.

## Pillar 1: Target Quality

Score whether the business itself is worth owning before considering price or Kay fit.

Core criteria:
- Business type: boring B2B/service, critical, repeat-use, non-retail, non-restaurant.
- Revenue quality: contractual, subscription, recurring, reoccurring, route-based, renewal-driven, or repeat relationship revenue. Separate from one-time project revenue.
- Retention/cohort durability: churn, renewal rates, customer tenure, cohort behavior, and evidence that demand survives owner transition.
- Margins: EBITDA margin floor is generally 15%+. Strong is 20%+.
- EBITDA scale: apply the G&B size lens above.
- Customer concentration: top customer under 15% is preferred. Top 5 concentration and public-company nuance should be captured.
- Market structure: fragmented supply, room for add-ons, non-commodity positioning, and defensible local or niche reputation.
- Growth and cyclicality: volume, pricing, one-time project effects, seasonality, and recession sensitivity.
- Operations and people: employee base, key-person dependency, route density or delivery model, licensing/regulatory requirements, and culture signals.

Target Quality red flags:
- EBITDA below $750K.
- Retail or restaurant category.
- Project revenue presented as recurring without evidence.
- Top customer above 15% without a credible durability explanation.
- Owner is the primary sales engine, technical expert, or customer relationship holder with no transition plan.
- Margins below 15% without a clear, low-risk improvement path.

## Pillar 2: Deal Structure

Score whether the transaction is economically feasible and financeable.

Core criteria:
- Purchase price and valuation: asking price, EBITDA multiple, revenue multiple when relevant, and valuation gap versus G&B return needs.
- Financing feasibility: SBA/bank/senior debt capacity, cash conversion, debt service coverage, and whether EBITDA converts into available cash.
- Working capital: target working capital, AR/AP patterns, seasonality, inventory, deferred revenue, and whether the seller is excluding needed operating cash.
- Capex: maintenance capex, fleet/equipment refresh, software or infrastructure spend, and hidden catch-up needs.
- Add-backs: owner comp, related-party rent, personal expenses, one-time items, wage normalization, and whether adjustments are credible.
- Seller financing: availability, size, terms, subordination, and whether it bridges valuation or transition risk.
- Rollover/earnout: whether the structure aligns incentives or hides overvaluation.
- Real estate: included/excluded, lease terms, landlord identity, market rent, relocation risk.
- Process pressure: LOI deadline, competitive process, broker behavior, and diligence access.

Deal Structure red flags:
- No asking price or valuation guidance when a near-term decision is required.
- Seller refuses normal working-capital or add-back support.
- EBITDA looks high but cash conversion is unclear.
- Financing depends on aggressive growth or unsupportable add-backs.
- Seller financing is unavailable where it is needed to bridge transition or valuation risk.

## Pillar 3: Buyer Fit

Score whether Kay/G&B should be the buyer, even if the business is attractive.

Core criteria:
- Kay fit: industry curiosity, operating credibility, ability to diligence, and willingness to spend years with the business.
- G&B fit: aligns with current theses, source strategy, women-led/network edge where relevant, and existing relationship channels.
- Investor fit: likely acceptable to Jeff/Guillermo/investor base based on scale, quality, downside risk, and strategic clarity.
- Execution risk: operational complexity, technical specialization, labor model, regulatory burden, integration risk, and support Kay would need.
- Transition risk: seller motivation, reason for sale, post-close role, second layer of management, and customer/employee transition risk.
- Strategic option value: platform potential, add-on availability, niche learning value, and whether relationship-only follow-up is valuable even if the deal is not.

Buyer Fit red flags:
- Kay lacks a believable path to operate or diligence the business.
- Investor story would require too much explanation for the deal's size or quality.
- Seller motivation is unclear or inconsistent.
- Execution risk is high and not compensated by business quality, price, or structure.

## Recommendation Rules

Proceed:
Use only when Target Quality is strong, Deal Structure is plausible, Buyer Fit is plausible, and no major diligence gap blocks next action.

Proceed but request more information:
Use when the deal appears promising but missing information could materially change the answer. Draft specific broker/seller questions.

Relationship-only:
Use when the deal is not a fit but the intermediary, seller, or source is worth cultivating for future flow.

Pass:
Use when a hard-no category, size floor, customer concentration, weak revenue quality, margin issue, valuation gap, or buyer-fit problem makes the deal unlikely to work.

Insufficient information:
Use when the package is too thin to make a reliable call. Do not score unknowns as neutral. Request the minimum data needed to screen: revenue, EBITDA/SDE, margins, revenue mix, customer concentration, years in business, owner role, reason for sale, asking price, and deal structure.

## Broker or Seller Questions

Ask only questions that would change the next decision. Default set:
- What are revenue, EBITDA/SDE, and EBITDA margin for the last 3 fiscal years and LTM?
- What percentage of revenue is recurring, contractual, reoccurring, project-based, or one-time?
- What are top customer and top 5 customer concentration?
- What add-backs are included in adjusted EBITDA, and can they be supported?
- What working-capital target is assumed in the transaction?
- What maintenance capex or equipment/software catch-up is required?
- What is the asking price or valuation expectation?
- Is seller financing, rollover, or an earnout expected or available?
- What is the seller's reason for selling and desired post-close role?
- Who owns key customer relationships and day-to-day operations today?

## Why This Matters for Kay

This section should be short and decision-oriented:
- Time cost: whether this deserves Kay's attention now.
- Downside: the biggest way this deal could waste time or fail.
- Upside: the specific reason it could be worth leaning in.
- Next action: the one step that unlocks the next decision.
