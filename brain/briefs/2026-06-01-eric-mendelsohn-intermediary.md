## AGENDA ITEMS

1. **Understand his deal flow.** "Walk me through the kinds of businesses you typically take to market: sectors, owner profile, deal sizes, and how many engagements you're running at a time." This tells you whether his pipeline overlaps your box and how exclusive his listings are.
2. **Establish how he likes to work with buyers.** Ask whether he sends curated opportunities to specific buyers he's qualified, or markets broadly. Principal-led + curated is the high-value pattern; if he runs broad auctions, calibrate expectations (LMM listings often reach many buyers).
3. **Convey your criteria at the right altitude.** Give him a clean, memorable buyer profile he can match against: profitable B2B/B2B2C service businesses, owner-operated, US (not CA), lower middle market, clean financials. Enough to filter, not enough to leak strategy.
4. **Market intel.** He sees live LMM seller sentiment daily. Ask what he's seeing on valuation expectations and which sectors have the most owner-readiness right now. Cross-reference the XPX/Axial data (silver-tsunami supply, buyer-stretch collapse 25% to 11%, AI-defensibility hurdle).

---

## OPTIONAL ITEMS

- **Reciprocal value:** You're an active, disciplined buyer with capital and a clear box, the kind of qualified buyer a sell-side advisor wants in his rolodex. Offer to be a fast, straight-shooting counterparty: quick yes/no, no tire-kicking. That is the currency he values most.
- **Women-led / female-owner businesses:** if he surfaces owner profiles, lean toward female-led or women-owned operators silently. Do not announce the lens.
- **Red flags to watch:** blast-only / mass-auction behavior (deal goes to thousands, low signal); deals concentrated below your floor; any conflict where he reps a business you'd compete with another buyer for. Note, don't react.
