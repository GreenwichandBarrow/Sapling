# MEETING BRIEF: Matthew Luczyk

Coffee (in-person) | Tuesday June 2, 2026, 11:00 AM EDT
Starbucks, 444 Madison Ave, NYC

---

## HOW YOU WERE INTRODUCED

You first met Matt in person at an XPX (Exit Planning Exchange) event. Afterward, his colleague Becky Wuest Creavin (Peapack Private, your connector into Cara Lovenson's Heels to Deals women's networking lunch) relayed on May 13 that Matt had "a great conversation with you at the XPX event and felt he had someone who might be a good fit for you." Matt was swamped at the time and slow to circle back, so Becky offered to nudge him. This coffee is the result of that follow-up. It is your first one-on-one with Matt, building on a brief but positive XPX exchange. There is no prior direct email correspondence on file, so treat this as a first real conversation, not a cold meeting.

---

## WHO MATT IS

- SVP and Head of Corporate Advisory at Peapack Private Investment Banking, the advisory arm of Peapack-Gladstone Bank (founded 1921, NY tri-state private bank).
- He advises business owners through selling a company, acquiring one, raising private growth capital, and building succession plans. In short, he is a sell-side / corporate M&A advisor to owners.
- He has appeared on the TV program "Minding Your Business" discussing the steps owners must take before selling or acquiring a business.
- His pitch is that Peapack pairs corporate advisory with private wealth management, so an owner gets both the deal and the post-sale wealth plan under one roof.
- Colleague of Becky Wuest Creavin; both sit inside the XPX / exit-planning advisory world.

---

## HOW HE COULD BE USEFUL TO YOUR SEARCH

1. **He is an intermediary sitting on owner deal flow.** As Head of Corporate Advisory, Matt represents owners who are selling. That is a direct sell-side channel. If a mandate fits your buy-box (B2B / B2B2C services, US, $2-10M EBITDA range, fragmented niche), he can put deals in front of you.
2. **He explicitly flagged a potential fit.** Becky relayed that Matt "has someone who might be a good fit for you." Surface this gently and let him tell you who he had in mind.
3. **Succession-planning network.** XPX and Peapack are wired into owners thinking about transition. That is precisely the population you want, and Matt is a natural referral node into it.
4. **Market intelligence.** He sees live valuations, multiples, and what is actually trading in the lower-middle market across the tri-state. Useful comp intel even when a specific deal is not a fit.

---

## WHAT TO SHARE ABOUT YOURSELF

- Matt already likes you from XPX, so lead warm and personal, not pitch-y.
- Position G&B simply: you are acquiring one strong, established lower-middle-market business to own and grow, backed by a group of investors. Keep it human and operator-flavored, not financial-engineering-flavored.
- It is appropriate to share your buy-box at a high level with an intermediary: B2B or B2B2C services, US-based, established (10+ years), the kind of operationally critical business an owner has spent a career building. You can mention the EBITDA / revenue band if he asks, since he is an advisor who needs it to match you to deals.
- Mutual value: you are a serious, well-capitalized buyer who closes and respects owners and their legacy. That is exactly the kind of buyer he wants in his rolodex for his sell-side mandates.
- Becky is your shared connection. A warm word about Becky and Heels to Deals reinforces the relationship.

---

## WHAT NOT TO OVER-SHARE

- Do not use the words "fund," "search fund," "committed equity," or "24-month window." With an intermediary, structural labels read as a competing buyer and a clock. Keep it to "a group of investors behind me" if capital comes up.
- Keep specific niche names and your thesis high-level. He is a broker who talks to many buyers; do not hand him your target list.
- Do not name your investors.
- No internal HoldCo / stage framing.
- Do not commit to anything on the spot. If he has a specific deal, the next step is an NDA, not a yes.

---

## SUGGESTED TALKING POINTS

1. **His Corporate Advisory practice.** "I'd love to hear how the Corporate Advisory group works at Peapack, and what you're seeing in the lower-middle market right now." Let him talk; it tells you his deal flow and where it sits.
2. **The "good fit" he mentioned.** "Becky mentioned you thought there might be someone who'd be a good fit for what I'm doing. I'd love to hear who you had in mind." This is the warmest, most concrete reason you are both here. Get the specific.
3. **Your search, briefly and warmly.** "I'm looking to acquire one established, operationally strong B2B services business in the US and grow it, with a group of investors behind me. I care a lot about the kind of owner and business it is, not just the numbers." Then let him react and start matching you to mandates.
4. **How to work together going forward.** "What's the best way for me to stay on your radar as deals come up that might fit?" Ask how he likes buyers to engage, and offer to be a clean, fast, owner-respecting buyer for his sell-side mandates.

---

## OBJECTIVE FOR THIS COFFEE

Turn a warm XPX hello into a working sell-side relationship: (1) get the specific "good fit" he flagged, (2) establish yourself as a serious, owner-respecting buyer he can route mandates to, and (3) agree on how he will surface deals to you going forward. Relationship first, deal second.
