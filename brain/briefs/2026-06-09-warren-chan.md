## HOW HE COULD BE USEFUL TO YOUR SEARCH

1. Warren can pressure-test whether art-world services are real recurring/referral-locked businesses or mostly bespoke relationship businesses.
2. His family-office / collector exposure may surface pain points around installation, crating, storage-adjacent services, customs, collection management, estate handling, and insurance.
3. His inbound artist-business opportunity can help distinguish what is not a fit from what might be structurally attractive.
4. His Anacapa/search-investor lens can help assess whether the services-only art thesis fits the search-fund model or drifts into too-small, too-taste-driven, or too-key-person-dependent territory.

## WHAT TO SHARE ABOUT YOURSELF

- Warren already knows Kay is interested in the intersection of the art world and acquisition/search models.
- Share that G&B has been studying art-world services, with a current bias toward operationally necessary services rather than pure advisory, art-tech, or single-artist businesses.
- Useful framing: the question is whether there is a services layer with recurring/referral-locked demand, fragmented owner-operated supply, and margins that can support a search acquisition.
- It is fine to mention examples at a category level: crating, fine-art logistics, installation/rigging, customs brokerage, collection services, and insurance-adjacent services.

---

## WHAT NOT TO OVER-SHARE

- Do not frame the art world as attractive because it is aesthetically interesting. Keep the thesis grounded in service economics and acquisition fit.
- Do not blur pure art advisory, artist businesses, art-tech, storage-heavy businesses, and logistics/services into one bucket.
- Avoid giving away G&B's full niche scorecard or target list.
- Do not overcommit to Fine-Art Logistics as a decided thesis. This call is validation and falsification.

---

## SUGGESTED TALKING POINTS

1. Start with Warren's inbound opportunity: what was the artist's business, why was it unusual, and what made him lean out?
2. Ask where serious collectors and family offices feel operational pain: movement, installation, insurance, customs, storage coordination, estate transitions, or collection administration.
3. Share G&B's current services-only hypothesis and ask him to attack it: is the asset-light services layer real, or are the attractive economics elsewhere?
4. Ask who he would speak with if he wanted to understand the operator side of art-world services, not just the collector or gallery side.
