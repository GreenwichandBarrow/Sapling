## WHAT TO PUSH FOR

The goal is to test the growth thesis, not over-disclose Kay's specific playbook.

- Ask Juan to describe the best customers in the business and why they stay. Listen for discretion, responsiveness, trust, reporting, urgency, and complexity.
- Ask which customer types have the most unmet need: luxury retail, hospitality, food halls, commercial real estate, property managers, corporate campuses, high-end multifamily, or restaurants.
- Ask whether Total Extermination wins because of service quality, relationships, technical competence, reporting, speed, price, or simply availability.
- Ask what a premium commercial customer wants that a standard pest company does not deliver well.
- Ask how much growth could come from the existing customer profile and referral network before needing acquisitions.
- Ask what back-end reporting customers actually value: compliance logs, trend reports, audit readiness, location-level reporting, response times, executive summaries, or proof of service.
- Ask whether trash compactors, trash rooms, waste areas, and sanitation-adjacent services create more pest stickiness or just create operational complexity.
- Ask for a customer concentration view: top 10 customers, percent of revenue, service cadence, contract length, renewal behavior, and churn history.
- Ask for technician utilization: number of techs, route density, spare capacity, hiring difficulty, training time, and whether the business can add routes without degrading service.
- Ask what Juan would need to believe to be excited about a sale, transition, or partnership.

Suggested phrasing for Kay's core question:

"One thing I am trying to understand is whether there is an organic growth opportunity in serving a more specific commercial customer profile, the type of client that really cares about discretion, response time, reporting, and reliability. Do you think that is a real wedge in pest, or do you think the market ultimately treats all providers the same?"

Suggested add-on question:

"I have also wondered whether trash compactor or trash-area service is naturally adjacent to pest for the right commercial clients. Is that a real operational opportunity, or would you view that as a distraction from the core route business?"
