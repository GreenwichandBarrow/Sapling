# Kay Search Preference Throughline

**Client:** [[entities/greenwich-and-barrow]]
**Timeline:** 2026-06-25 -> 2026-06-25
**Linear:** null

## Executive Summary

Kay's strongest acquisition pull is not toward a broad industry label. It is toward stewardship services for high-value, emotionally significant assets and premium environments where trust, discretion, taste, technical expertise, and operational excellence matter.

This surfaced during review of the 232-lane thesis inventory, especially after Kay connected her long-running attachment to the Josiah Wedgwood story in *Creating Modern Capitalism* with her desire to inherit and grow a real company: a business with heritage, craft, credibility, customer trust, and enough substance to improve over decades.

The search-fund constraint remains real: the business must still fit target density, size, owner access, and multiple requirements. But future niche work should treat this throughline as a primary qualitative filter, not as soft color.

## Key Patterns

- Pattern 1: Kay is drawn to businesses where trust is the product. The business should protect, preserve, insure, move, maintain, or discreetly support something customers genuinely care about.
- Pattern 2: Kay responds to high-value object worlds: fine art, collectibles, jewelry, yachts, couture, luxury retail environments, and other premium tangible categories. The right expression is service infrastructure around those objects, not retail or product ownership.
- Pattern 3: Kay wants a Wedgwood-style operating canvas: an existing company with good bones, customer trust, and room for continuous improvements in service quality, brand credibility, process, distribution, reporting, and customer experience.
- Pattern 4: Kay does not want retail, inventory exposure, DTC fashion cycles, or a product-line startup, even though her old desire to create high-end sweatshirts reveals attraction to premium object, taste, brand, and quality worlds.
- Pattern 5: Relationship-driven, expert-led, slightly nerdy industries energize Kay more than generic operating-grind businesses. She likes circles where lawyers, bankers, advisors, collectors, family offices, specialists, and sophisticated customers matter.
- Pattern 6: Expert enthusiasm does not equal sourcing validation. A thesis is not exhausted until Kay has run a deliberate owner-access motion, including direct calls where appropriate.
- Pattern 7: The best-fitting niches so far were fine art storage and related services, art/collectibles/jewelry/yacht insurance, and luxury-boutique/discreet commercial pest. Each matched the trust/discretion/premium-service pattern, but each struggled with target density, size, multiples, or owner access under search criteria.

## Lessons Learned

Kay's strongest industry preferences so far:

1. **Fine art storage and related services**
   - Multiple revenue streams.
   - Trust-based with cherished works of art.
   - Clear operational inefficiencies Kay could see and improve.
   - Premium service and discretion matter.

2. **Art / collectibles / jewelry / yacht insurance brokerage**
   - Nerdy/expert knowledge component.
   - Proximity to art and high-value objects.
   - Many people in the business genuinely love art or the underlying objects.
   - Potential coverage areas include handbags, haute couture, jewelry, collections, yachts, and other worlds Kay knows and can discuss credibly with clientele.
   - Remote/portable and relationship-driven.

3. **Luxury-boutique / discreet commercial pest**
   - Opportunity to bring discretion, reporting, responsiveness, and service quality to an industry that does not currently present that way.
   - Organic growth angle through premium commercial customers.
   - Less emotionally resonant than art/insurance, but the service-quality elevation pattern is relevant.

What has not worked yet:

- These preferred lanes have not yet produced enough right-sized targets, workable multiples, or owner access.
- Insurance was not truly exhausted; Kay had zero owner conversations and mostly reached large strategics, experts, carriers, lawyers, bankers, and advisors. The failed test was light-touch relationship-led sourcing, not necessarily the industry itself.
- Future insurance work should distinguish generic brokerage roll-up from specialty high-value-asset insurance and insurance-adjacent services.

## Application

Use this preference lens when evaluating new niches:

- Does the business touch high-value assets, cherished objects, premium environments, or sophisticated customers?
- Is trust, discretion, taste, expertise, or quality meaningfully valued by the buyer?
- Is there a service/brand/process layer Kay could improve over time?
- Is the business close enough to Kay's natural circles that she can show up credibly without forcing a costume?
- Does the business still satisfy search-fund math: target density, target size, owner access, recurring/reoccurring revenue, and workable multiples?

Do not overfit to art, insurance, or luxury labels. The real pattern is stewardship of high-value assets and premium environments through expert, trusted, operationally improvable services.

## Screening Doctrine Update - 2026-06-25

When evaluating a new niche idea that fits Kay's Wedgwood / stewardship / trust-based premium-services pattern, do not score everything immediately. Use a two-stage screen.

### Stage 1 - Hard Initial Screen

A lane must first pass two practical gates:

1. **Revenue quality:** The business cannot be purely transactional or one-off project work. It needs recurring, reoccurring, contracted, mandated, replenishment, maintenance, renewal, insurance/estate-triggered, or otherwise repeatable revenue.
2. **Acquirability / target density:** There must be plausible opportunities in the market to acquire: enough owner-led companies, some right-sized targets, and a credible path to owner access. If target density is clearly too thin, the lane should not receive a full score no matter how strong the emotional or strategic fit is.

If either gate fails, mark the lane as **No-Go / Watch Only** and document why.

### Stage 2 - Full Score Only If Stage 1 Passes

Only after passing Stage 1 should the system score the remaining criteria:

- Kay energy / Wedgwood fit
- customer diversity
- mission criticality / consequence of failure
- service-quality differentiation
- right-to-win / network access
- search-math risk beyond the two hard gates

This prevents the system from over-scoring elegant but non-acquirable niches such as overly narrow art-world subcategories.
