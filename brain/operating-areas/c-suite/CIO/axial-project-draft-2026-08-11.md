# Recommendation

Create narrow Axial projects around G&B's luxury-adjacent HoldCo architecture, not generic search-fund services categories. The first wave should focus on niches that are targetable in the U.S., likely to produce real cash flow, and tied to high-trust luxury, wealth, asset-care, beauty/longevity, or premium built-environment demand.

Art Storage and Specialty Insurance Brokerage are excluded because they are already live in Axial.

Recommended first wave:

1. Luxury Home Technology Service & Integration
2. Wine Storage, Cellar Management & Collection Care
3. Fine Art Installation, Conservation Support & Collections Care
4. Aesthetic & Wellness Equipment Service
5. Estate Inventory, Appraisal Support & Personal Property Documentation
6. Jewelry, Watch & Luxury Collectibles Repair / Authentication Infrastructure
7. Premium Retail Store Care, Fixture Repair & Brand Environment Maintenance
8. Customs, Documentation & Compliance for Luxury / Specialty Goods

Second wave / monitor:

1. Medical Spa / Aesthetic Practice Compliance & Training Services
2. Private Club, Luxury Hospitality & Independent School Facility Care
3. Profitable Vertical Software for Galleries, Private Clubs, Marinas, or HNW Services
4. Premium Rental Infrastructure for Luxury Events, Showrooms & Hospitality

# Evidence / Assumptions

G&B is building toward a luxury-adjacent holding company / LDE, not a broad business-services search. Axial projects should therefore point brokers toward narrow categories connected to luxury credibility, asset and wealth protection, storage/custody/care, beauty/wellness/longevity infrastructure, premium built environments, and profitable workflow-critical vertical software.

Working assumptions:

- Deal 1 must stand alone financially and remain legible to search investors.
- Target businesses should have real cash flow, attractive ROIC, repeat or recurring demand, and sensible entry multiples.
- Luxury adjacency is useful only when it improves sourcing, right-to-win, customer durability, referral networks, or margin quality.
- Broad categories such as pest, stormwater, fire safety, and industrial machinery are too generic unless narrowed to premium/luxury/wealth/asset-protection use cases.
- Vertical software is acceptable only when profitable, niche, workflow-critical, and not venture-style SaaS.

# Open Questions

1. Which Axial projects can be supported by enough U.S. target density without broadening into generic categories?
2. Which niches can credibly produce a Deal 1 in the $750K-$3M EBITDA review zone, or ideally $3M+ EBITDA?
3. Which categories produce recurring revenue versus merely repeat project demand?
4. Which niches have buyer/seller language that brokers understand without diluting the luxury-adjacent thesis?
5. Which projects should be held back to avoid Axial alert noise?
6. For software/data projects, what minimum profitability and churn profile should be required before review?

# Final Proposed Project Language

## 1. Luxury Home Technology Service & Integration

### Short Acquisition Thesis

Acquire a high-end smart-home, AV, lighting, shade, network, and security integration business serving HNW homes, estates, architects, designers, builders, and estate managers, with repeat service, maintenance, monitoring, upgrade, and referral-driven revenue.

### Include Criteria

- Luxury residential technology integrators
- Crestron, Lutron, Savant, Control4, AV, lighting, shades, networking, and security integration
- Maintenance retainers, service contracts, upgrades, and ongoing support
- Repeat referral sources from architects, builders, interior designers, property managers, and estate managers
- Real cash flow and technician bench beyond the founder

### Exclude Criteria

- Low-end security installers
- Electricians with occasional smart-home work
- DTC smart-home device retailers
- Project-only installers with no service base
- Venture-style software or hardware companies

### Keywords / Synonyms / Broker-Friendly Language

Luxury home automation, residential technology integrator, smart home integrator, estate technology service, AV integration, home theater installation, lighting control, shade automation, Crestron dealer, Lutron dealer, Savant integrator, Control4 integrator.

### Red Flags

- No service contracts or maintenance retainers
- Founder owns all referrals and client relationships
- Project backlog masking weak recurring revenue
- Weak technician bench
- Warranty/callback burden
- Heavy dependence on one platform or one builder relationship

### Why This Fits G&B

Direct HNW and luxury built-environment infrastructure. The installed base can create repeat service revenue, and trust/referral networks matter.

### Deal 1 Fit

Inference. Very on-brand and potentially cash-flowing, but recurring service depth must be proven.
