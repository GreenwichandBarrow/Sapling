# 03 - G&B Capital Structure Decision Tree

Status: Complete for strategic comparison; legal/tax structuring requires counsel.

Purpose: compare traditional search, Deal 1 plus parallel HoldCo raise, independent sponsor, SBA/personal acquisition, and startup-first against Kay's long-term objective: build a private, luxury-adjacent holding company with meaningful ownership, cash flow, control, and a path to personal liquidity.

## Decision Criteria

- Ownership: what Kay owns directly or through her personal vehicle.
- Control: who can remove Kay or block decisions.
- Salary: whether Kay can earn at least $300K.
- Investor oversight: how much governance comes with the capital.
- Path to $10M personal liquidity: realistic route to cash, not just paper value.
- Risk: personal guarantee, career risk, timing risk, capital risk.
- HoldCo fit: alignment with the long-term G&B vision.

## Option A: Traditional Search Deal 1 Only

Ownership:
- Fact: Traditional search economics commonly give the searcher a meaningful but minority promoted equity stake, often tied to close, time vesting, and performance.
- Inference: Kay can likely target 25% to 30% searcher economics if the deal clears investor underwriting.

Control:
- Fact: Search investors typically retain board control and can remove the CEO.
- Inference: Kay has operating authority but not ultimate control.

Salary:
- Fact: Salary is usually paid by the acquired company and must fit the company's post-close budget.
- Inference: A $300K salary is feasible only if the target has sufficient EBITDA and lender/investor comfort.

Investor Oversight:
- High.

Path to $10M Personal Liquidity:
- Inference: Usually requires a successful exit in 5 to 7 years or meaningful distributions along the way.
- Constraint: One healthy company may still not create $10M personal cash if entry multiple, exit multiple, debt, preferred return, and growth are not strong enough.

Risk:
- Medium. Lower personal cash risk, higher career/control risk.

HoldCo Fit:
- Medium. It can be a bridge, but it does not by itself create the permanent-capital G&B architecture.

Best Use:
- If Deal 1 is financeable and strong enough to prove Kay can buy, lead, and create value.

## Option B: Deal 1 Plus Parallel HoldCo / Long-Duration Enterprise Raise

Ownership:
- Hypothesis: Kay could negotiate founder equity in the HoldCo, potentially 20% to 30% with additional upside through performance, acquisition, or promote-like economics.
- Fact: Stanford's 2026 search study introduced long-duration enterprises as a distinct category and identified 67 such vehicles.

Control:
- Inference: More flexible than traditional search if designed correctly, but governance depends on documents.
- Risk: If investors control the HoldCo board, Kay may still be removable or constrained.

Salary:
- Inference: Salary could be paid at HoldCo level, operating company level, or split, depending on structure and capitalization.
- Kay requirement: at least $300K.

Investor Oversight:
- Medium to high, depending on investor count, board rights, reserved matters, and reporting obligations.

Path to $10M Personal Liquidity:
- Inference: More plausible than a single deal if G&B can own multiple companies, compound equity value, generate distributions, and potentially create partial founder liquidity through recapitalization or secondary sale.
- Constraint: This is not automatic. It depends on the ownership percentage, acquisition pace, debt capacity, free cash flow, and whether investors permit founder liquidity before exit.

Risk:
- Medium-high. More complex to raise and govern, but better aligned if Kay truly wants to own businesses rather than operate one company indefinitely.

HoldCo Fit:
- High.

Best Use:
- If Deal 1 is financially credible enough to close and strategically distinctive enough to justify a broader permanent-capital platform.

## Option C: Independent Sponsor

Ownership:
- Inference: Kay could negotiate economics deal by deal, potentially more flexibly than traditional search.

Control:
- Inference: Control varies by deal. Capital providers may still control governance.

Salary:
- Inference: Less predictable. Fees and salary must be negotiated per transaction.

Investor Oversight:
- Medium to high per deal, but less permanent than a HoldCo.

Path to $10M Personal Liquidity:
- Hypothesis: Possible through strong deal economics across several deals, but less stable without committed capital.

Risk:
- High execution risk. Every deal requires a new capital raise.

HoldCo Fit:
- Medium. It can become a HoldCo over time, but it does not solve capital availability upfront.

Best Use:
- If traditional search investors will not back the chosen category but individual backers will fund a specific transaction.

## Option D: SBA / Personal Acquisition

Ownership:
- Fact: SBA 7(a) loans can finance ownership changes and have a maximum loan amount of $5M.
- Fact: As of July 4, 2026, qualified borrowers who first secure SBA 7(a) financing may access up to $5M 7(a) plus up to $5M 504 for a combined $10M in SBA-backed financing.
- Inference: SBA could allow Kay to own more of Deal 1 personally if she qualifies.

Control:
- High operating control, subject to lender covenants and personal guarantee obligations.

Salary:
- Inference: Salary can be flexible if the company can support it, but lender underwriting matters.

Investor Oversight:
- Lower than institutional search investors.

Path to $10M Personal Liquidity:
- Inference: Higher personal ownership helps, but personal guarantee and debt risk are material.

Risk:
- High personal financial risk. Kay has stated current personal financial position makes this difficult.

HoldCo Fit:
- High if feasible, because it preserves ownership/control.
- Practical fit: currently low unless creditworthiness, equity injection, and guarantee constraints can be solved.

Best Use:
- Attractive in theory, constrained in practice.

## Option E: Startup-First

Ownership:
- High.

Control:
- High.

Salary:
- Low to uncertain in the near term.

Investor Oversight:
- Low if self-funded; high if venture or angel-backed.

Path to $10M Personal Liquidity:
- Hypothesis: possible only with strong product-market fit, growth, and exit or profitable scale.

Risk:
- High timing risk. It does not solve the current search runway unless income or funding is secured.

HoldCo Fit:
- Low to medium. A startup can become a platform, but it delays the ownership path and does not leverage the already-raised search base.

Best Use:
- Best as a parallel idea lab, not the primary six-month path unless the search path collapses.

## Recommended Direction

The best-fit structure appears to be Option B: Deal 1 plus a parallel HoldCo / long-duration vehicle raise, with a smaller aligned investor base if possible.

That recommendation is an inference, not a fact. It depends on:
- Deal 1 being financeable on its own.
- Deal 1 supporting the broader G&B story.
- Governance protecting Kay's owner-architect role.
- Salary being explicit.
- Kay owning meaningful HoldCo economics, not only searcher equity in one asset.
- Investors agreeing that the platform, not just the first acquisition, is the underwriting object.
