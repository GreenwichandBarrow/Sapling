# 04 - SG Pressure Test Without Romance

Status: Partial.

Reason for Partial: this can be pressure-tested with current facts, but it cannot be fully concluded without seller-provided channel revenue, gross margin by channel, inventory aging/liquidation support, customer concentration, lease terms, working-capital needs, and post-close management plan.

Purpose: assume Sidney Garber is not special and evaluate whether it can clear a traditional search investor bar despite retail, B2C, and inventory risk.

## Known Facts

- Fact: SG is a legacy luxury jewelry brand founded in 1946.
- Fact: The seller is 76 and is considering transition options.
- Fact: The business has a Madison Avenue store in New York and another store in Chicago.
- Fact: The business has ecommerce and wholesale accounts.
- Fact: Kay has stated wholesale is approximately 31% of the business.
- Fact: Kay received EBITDA of approximately $3.3M to $3.4M.
- Fact: Kay has discussed a likely valuation range around $10M to $12M depending on structure.
- Fact: Jeff Stevens framed the opportunity as outside the classic search lane and said the inventory question is central.
- Fact: Guillermo Lavergne encouraged Kay not to dismiss a nontraditional deal if she has strong conviction and can prove the growth case.
- Fact: Andrew Freiman viewed the jewelry lead as a serious diligence path but said the HoldCo vision should remain secondary until a first acquisition exists.
- Fact: Andrew Saltoun viewed the opportunity as potentially worth diligence if Kay validates growth, inventory economics, and structure.

## Traditional Search Bar

SG clears some search criteria and fails or weakens others.

### Criteria It Appears to Clear

- Size: Fact. EBITDA above $3M is meaningful for a traditional search acquisition.
- Seller motivation: Fact. Seller is actively considering transition.
- Kay fit: Fact. Kay has luxury, brand, Chanel, strategy, and relationship-building fit.
- Differentiation: Inference. A heritage jewelry brand may give Kay a proprietary angle relative to generic investors.
- Possible valuation discipline: Hypothesis. A $10M to $12M valuation on $3.3M to $3.4M EBITDA would be approximately 3.0x to 3.6x EBITDA, but this depends on what EBITDA truly is after normalization and working capital.

### Criteria It Does Not Clearly Clear

- Contracted recurring revenue: Fact. Jewelry revenue is primarily transactional unless repair, service, wholesale reorder behavior, or membership-like programs are proven.
- B2C exposure: Fact. Retail/ecommerce are B2C channels.
- Inventory risk: Fact. Jewelry requires inventory, valuation discipline, and working-capital management.
- Key-person / creative risk: Inference. Seller and brand story matter, and the post-close creative/design engine must be understood.
- Exit buyer universe: Inference. Search investors may see a narrower buyer pool than healthcare, software, or B2B services.
- Cyclicality: Fact at category level. Luxury is cyclical; jewelry may be more resilient than fashion, but it is not non-cyclical.

## What Would Make It Investable

SG does not need to look like HerScan. It needs to show a credible search return path despite being a different kind of asset.

The strongest investability case would be:

1. The purchase price is disciplined.
   - Fact: Kay is considering a $10M to $12M valuation range.
   - Inference: A 3x to 4x EBITDA entry price could compensate for nontraditional search risk if EBITDA quality is real.

2. Cash conversion is real.
   - Fact: EBITDA alone is not owner cash flow.
   - Required evidence: working capital, inventory reinvestment, maintenance capex, cash taxes, normalized compensation, and consultant cost normalization.

3. Inventory is downside protection, not a trap.
   - Required evidence: aging schedule, resale/liquidation value, SKU productivity, obsolete inventory, melt value where relevant, consignment exposure, and lender willingness to underwrite inventory.

4. Growth is not only "luxury is growing."
   - Required evidence: specific account expansion, ecommerce funnel improvement, CRM/clienteling improvement, product category growth, trunk shows, retail productivity, wholesale reorder behavior, and marketing ROI.

5. The operating plan does not depend entirely on Kay.
   - Required evidence: Camilla role, finance function, merchandising/inventory capability, creative/design continuity, store leadership, ecommerce support, and external advisor bench.

6. The HoldCo argument is additive, not defensive.
   - Inference: SG could be the cultural anchor for a future luxury infrastructure HoldCo, but investors should not be asked to fund SG only because it tells a better story.

## Growth Levers to Test

### Wholesale Expansion

Hypothesis: SG may have underdeveloped wholesale potential if the brand can add high-quality doors without diluting positioning.

Targets Kay mentioned:
- Saks New York
- Bloomingdale's New York
- London Jewelers East Hampton
- London Jewelers Americana

Evidence needed:
- Current wholesale sell-through.
- Margin by wholesale account.
- Reorder rates.
- Whether wholesale is true purchase, consignment, trunk show, or hybrid.
- Whether target accounts would carry brand at attractive terms.

### Ecommerce and Digital Clienteling

Inference: If SG has strong brand but underbuilt data capture, ecommerce, CRM, and clienteling may create growth without opening more stores.

Evidence needed:
- Traffic, conversion, AOV, repeat purchase, email/SMS list quality, CAC, paid media history, abandoned cart, customer segmentation.

### Repair, Redesign, and Heirloom Services

Hypothesis: SG can create repeat/reoccurring revenue and deepen trust through repair, redesign, and heirloom transformation.

Evidence needed:
- Existing repair/redesign volume.
- Gross margin and labor requirements.
- Whether the service is scalable beyond founder taste.
- Whether affluent women want this from SG specifically.

### CRM and Client Data Capture

Inference: If SG has underutilized client data, improved CRM can increase repeat purchase, gifting reminders, private appointments, trunk shows, and lifecycle occasions.

Evidence needed:
- Client database size and quality.
- Repeat purchase frequency.
- Top-client concentration.
- Store associate clienteling process.

### Expense Professionalization

Fact: Kay has noted significant consultant usage and believes some functions can be brought in-house.

Required evidence:
- Which consultant fees are recurring vs project.
- Which fees replace needed full-time roles.
- Which savings are real after hiring Camilla / finance / operating support.

## What Evidence Would Change the Answer to Yes

- Five-year financials show EBITDA quality, not one-year noise.
- Gross margin and EBITDA margin are strong versus branded fine jewelry peers.
- Inventory supports lender confidence and seller downside protection.
- Wholesale growth can be demonstrated with credible account conversations or comparable account economics.
- Ecommerce and CRM data show clear, fixable under-optimization.
- Seller accepts a structure with seller note and transition support.
- Investors accept that SG is Deal 1 inside a broader HoldCo, not a conventional recurring-revenue search deal.

## What Evidence Would Change the Answer to No

- EBITDA is dependent on owner undercompensation, unusual charity treatment, or non-recurring add-backs.
- Inventory is stale, overvalued, or illiquid.
- Wholesale is low-margin consignment with weak sell-through.
- Stores require high rent and high working capital without strong four-wall economics.
- Growth requires heavy marketing spend with unclear payback.
- The brand depends too much on seller's taste, relationships, or personal presence.
- Investors will only fund at a structure that leaves Kay without enough ownership, salary, or control to pursue the HoldCo vision.

## Bottom Line

SG is not a clean traditional search deal. It is a potentially investable nontraditional deal if the price is low, the cash conversion is real, the inventory supports downside protection, and the growth plan is proven with channel-level evidence.

Current conviction level: not enough for "HerScan-level" conviction yet.

Reasonable next step: make a disciplined offer or options map only if it is explicitly positioned as a path to deeper diligence, not as final conviction.
