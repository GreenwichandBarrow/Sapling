## Highest-Value Investor Sequence

1. Guillermo Lavergne

Why:
- Fact: Guillermo is one of Kay's two lead investors.
- Fact: Guillermo used the Cici McMullin / HerScan example to make the point that nontraditional deals can work when the searcher has real conviction and a compelling growth case.
- Inference: He may be open if Kay can show discipline, not romance.

Likely objections:
- Retail/B2C and transactional revenue.
- Whether growth is strong enough to justify leaving classic search criteria.
- Whether Kay's conviction is evidence-based.

Evidence needed:
- Channel-level growth plan.
- EBITDA quality and cash conversion.
- Inventory support.
- Seller structure.
- Clear explanation of why this is not just a luxury brand, but the first asset in a broader G&B architecture.

2. Sara Rosenthal

Why:
- Fact: Sara has already engaged on the luxury-jewelry path and the idea that inventory-backed jewelry behaves differently than fashion retail.
- Inference: She may be useful in pressure-testing how to explain the deal to a search audience.

Likely objections:
- Narrow buyer pool.
- Inventory value.
- How much of the business is true brand equity versus retail exposure.

Evidence needed:
- Inventory aging.
- Channel margins.
- Customer repeat behavior.
- Wholesale account quality.

3. Andrew Saltoun

Why:
- Fact: Andrew discussed SBA/personal acquisition as an attractive conceptual path and has been constructive on deal structure.
- Inference: He may help Kay think through ownership/control tradeoffs and alternative financing.

Likely objections:
- Whether the search-fund capital base is the right match.
- Whether Kay is overbuilding the HoldCo story before closing Deal 1.
- Whether SG is financeable without too much personal risk.

Evidence needed:
- Purchase price discipline.
- Seller note.
- Debt support.
- HoldCo economics and governance.

4. Andrew Freiman

Why:
- Fact: Andrew viewed the jewelry lead as a serious diligence path and said the first acquisition should remain the near-term focus.
- Inference: He may be open to the thesis if the HoldCo story does not distract from Deal 1 execution.

Likely objections:
- Too much platform narrative too early.
- Need for clean financials and inventory diligence.

Evidence needed:
- Simple offer structure.
- Diligence request list.
- SG-only underwriting case before broader HoldCo vision.

5. Will Bressman

Why:
- Fact: Will identified the central tension as whether Kay wants to own/operate this consumer/luxury business and whether investors support a modified path.
- Inference: He can help separate "buy question" from "operate question."

Likely objections:
- Kay's operating fit.
- Investor support.
- Consumer risk.

Evidence needed:
- Clear CEO / Camilla / advisor role map.
- Growth plan with measurable levers.
- Board-ready risk mitigants.

6. Jeff Stevens

Why not first:
- Fact: Jeff framed the deal as outside classic search and worth pressure-testing more as SBA/small self-funded than as a clean search-fund deal.
- Inference: Jeff is likely to be the toughest traditional-search screen.

Best use:
- Use after Kay has financial support, inventory support, and a structure.
- Treat Jeff as the "what could kill this?" investor, not as the first persuasion target.

Likely objections:
- Retail.
- B2C.
- Inventory.
- Not recurring.
- Narrow buyer pool.
- Whether this is a distraction from a cleaner search asset.

Evidence needed:
- Inventory liquidation support.
- Working capital model.
- Debt/lender feedback.
- Exit buyer universe.
- Proof that the HoldCo approach is not a way to justify a weak Deal 1.

## Investor Message Architecture

For SG:
- "This is not a clean recurring-revenue search deal. I am treating it as a disciplined exception only if the numbers, inventory, and growth evidence support it."

For HoldCo:
- "The long-term strategy is to build G&B as a focused luxury-adjacent holding company. Deal 1 must be underwritten on its own and must also make the platform more credible."

For risk:
- "The burden of proof is higher because the category is B2C and transactional. I am not asking anyone to ignore that. I am trying to determine whether price, cash flow, inventory, and growth offset it."

## Bottom Line

The investor path should not start by asking the most traditional investor to bless SG. It should start with the investors most likely to engage in a disciplined nontraditional case, then use the toughest investors to pressure-test what can kill the deal.
