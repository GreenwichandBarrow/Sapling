# Private Family Office Library Shelves

## Decision

Use eight working library shelves for Private Family Office:

1. **Household Finance**  
   Cash flow, bills, accounts, taxes, subscriptions, payroll, savings, debt.

2. **Risk & Protection**  
   Insurance, legal documents, estate basics, government IDs, emergency files, passwords, fraud, continuity planning.

3. **Home Operations**  
   Property, utilities, maintenance, repairs, vendors, supplies, warranties, inventory.

4. **Children & Dependents**  
   School, childcare, medical forms, activities, guardianship, elder care if relevant, routines.

5. **Health & Environment**  
   Doctors, medications, nutrition, food systems, allergies, records, mental health, air/water quality, safety.

6. **Travel & Mobility**  
   Passports, itineraries, packing systems, travel insurance, medical travel prep, transportation, house coverage while away.

7. **Time & Calendar**  
   Family calendar, recurring obligations, school-year rhythm, holidays, seasonal resets, weekly reviews.

8. **Giving & Community**  
   Donations, volunteering, school/community obligations, causes, family values, donor records.

## Current Interpretation

These are working shelf labels, not final brand language. The architecture is meant to let a new head of household scan the homepage and understand the major areas of family and household responsibility she now needs to manage.

## Facts

- Private Family Office is separate from Greenwich & Barrow, Kay's search fund, Chief of Staff work, and personal family-office planning.
- The venture is a self-serve digital library, not a consulting or custom-setup service.
- The primary audience is women becoming heads of household, often while serving as breadwinner and still carrying household-management expectations.
- Professionals and service providers should appear inside the relevant shelf rather than as a standalone shelf.
- Household operations belong under Home Operations.
- Food belongs under Health & Environment.
- Practical continuity and legacy-adjacent items currently belong under Risk & Protection.

## Assumptions

- Eight shelves will feel more complete and visually balanced than seven on a homepage library.
- The shelf architecture should represent household domains rather than vendor categories or life stages.
- The labels should lean academic and reference-library-like rather than cute, influencer-coded, luxury-coded, or overly editorial.

## Hypotheses

- This shelf set can act as the first homepage information architecture.
- The repeated book-opening pattern can make each shelf feel like a serious reference system rather than a blog feed.
- Each book can use a consistent internal structure: plain-English field guide, checklist, recommended tools/products/services, templates/downloads, and when to call a professional.

## Open Questions

- Whether the public shelf names should stay this literal or receive more polished academic labels.
- Which shelf should contain the first free download.
- Which shelf should contain the first paid kit.
