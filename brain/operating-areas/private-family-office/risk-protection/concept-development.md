Date: September 6, 2026

## Purpose

This document captures concept decisions specific to the **Risk & Protection** book inside The Private Family Office.

Venture-wide rules, brand rules, distribution decisions, and operating principles belong in the main **The Private Family Office - Concept Decisions** document. Book-specific structure, envelope names, page interactions, content scope, and product ideas for Risk & Protection belong here.

## Book Role

**Risk & Protection** is the first book to build for the MVP.

## Approved Opening Paragraph

The Private Family Office is an organization system for the process-oriented person managing the financial and operational responsibilities of a modern family office. The library is designed to be built over time and used in the same way: section by section, as each part of the system becomes ready for more order. You can begin anywhere, but we recommend starting with Risk & Protection, the first pillar in building a private family office for the modern home.

## Envelope Interaction

The Risk & Protection page should have two states:

- **Browse state:** envelopes appear larger as the main visual interface.
- **Reading state:** once a visitor clicks an envelope, the envelopes shrink into a compact left-side navigator, potentially as a scrolling list, while the right side becomes the primary reading/action pane.

The selected envelope should remain visibly active.

## Content Pattern Inside Each Envelope

Each envelope should open into a right-side panel or page area with:

- A plain-English explanatory paragraph.
- A visible checklist.
- Recommended Resources, including products, people, professional services, public resources, and secure-storage recommendations where relevant.
- A downloadable version of the checklist or related tool, available through email capture when appropriate.
- Review Rhythm.

Do not use **Where This Connects** as a standing section. If a connection to another envelope is useful, mention it naturally inside the relevant paragraph or recommendation area.

## Review Rhythm

Each envelope should note how often the documents or information inside should be reviewed or updated.

This review rhythm should support future reminder emails for subscribers.

## Identity Documents Content Rule

### Decision

For the **Identity Documents** envelope, do not use one blanket review rhythm for the entire envelope. The timing depends on the document itself.

The checklist should primarily show the kinds of documents that belong in the envelope. Where useful, each document type can include its own review or update note.

### Operating Implication

## Identity Documents Content Refinement

### Decision

For the **Identity Documents** envelope, the **Documents to Include** checklist should list the document names only. Explanatory guidance should live in the paragraph, protected-storage recommendation, or product recommendation area rather than inside the checklist itself.

### Protected Storage Recommendation

Protected physical storage is one of the main recommendations for this envelope, not a side note. The **Savor Folio Document Organizer** can be recommended as a physical home for documents that should be kept safely and located easily.

Documents that may belong in a protected physical organizer include passports, birth certificates, Social Security cards, marriage certificates, adoption records, immigration or citizenship documents, legal identity records, and insurance documents. A driver license usually would not belong in the organizer because it is normally carried day to day, though copies or related records may be stored if useful.

### Insurance Document Overlap

Insurance documents may appear in the protected-storage recommendation because they are often kept physically with other important papers. However, **Insurance Policies** should remain its own envelope because insurance needs deeper treatment: policy types, coverage review, renewal dates, carrier contacts, claims information, and household changes that should trigger a review.

### Operating Implication

The Identity Documents page should distinguish between:

- **Visible Checklist:** a clean list of document names, grouped by how they are managed.
- **Recommended Resources:** where the site can explain protected storage and link to the personally vetted Savor product.
- **Download:** the checklist should be visible on the page and also available as a downloadable version through email capture.

Insurance papers may be stored physically with important documents, but the full insurance system still lives under **Insurance Policies**.

## Identity Documents Checklist Structure

### Decision

The first Identity Documents checklist should not be a flat document list. It should separate identity records into two practical categories:

1. **Documents you receive or create once**
2. **Documents you need to renew or keep current**

### Rationale

This structure is more useful than a general inventory because the household manages these records differently. Some documents primarily need protected storage. Others require expiration tracking, renewal planning, or updates after a move, name change, travel need, or family-status change.

### Operating Implication

The Identity Documents envelope should use this two-part structure in both the web page and the downloadable checklist.

## Identity Documents Interface: Secure-Storage Icon

### Decision

On the website, use a small secure-storage icon after each document name when that document is recommended for protected physical storage.

The document name should come first, followed by the icon. The symbol should feel secure rather than merely administrative. A safe, lock, or lock-and-key symbol may be a better fit than a folder.

### Rationale

This makes the storage recommendation visible at the document level without adding explanatory text beside every checklist item. It also connects the checklist to the personally vetted Savor Folio recommendation in a practical, visual way.

### Operating Implication

The Identity Documents checklist can be organized into two groups: **Documents You Receive or Create Once** and **Documents You Need to Renew or Keep Current**. Items that should usually be stored in a protected physical organizer should show the secure-storage icon after the document name. Items that are normally carried day to day, such as a driver license, should not show the icon for the original document.

A short legend should explain the icon, for example: **Secure storage: keep the original or current copy in a protected household document organizer.**

## Envelope Content Structure Refinement

### Decision

Use a simplified envelope content structure. Secure storage should sit under **Recommended Resources**, not as its own standing section. The checklist should be visible on the page and also available as a downloadable asset through email capture. Do not use **Where This Connects** as a standard section.

2. **Checklist**
3. **Recommended Resources**
4. **Download**

### Rationale

This keeps the page clearer for the reader. The checklist is the useful public content; the download gives readers a practical asset to keep. Recommended Resources can hold products, people, services, government links, storage guidance, and personally vetted recommendations without fragmenting the page.

## Printable Content Rule: Checklist Only for First Free Download

### Decision

The first downloadable **Identity Documents Checklist** should be a true checklist only. It should not include the **Recommended Resources** section.

### Rationale

The free printable should be simple, useful, and easy to complete. Recommended Resources belong on the website page, where they can be explained in context and updated over time.

### Operating Implication

The printable may include the secure-storage icon legend because it explains how to use the checklist. Product recommendations, including the Savor Folio Document Organizer, should remain on the website page rather than inside this first downloadable checklist.

## Printable Checklist Interface: Inline Review Rhythm

### Decision

For the downloadable **Identity Documents Checklist**, show the review rhythm inline beside each relevant document type rather than as a separate large section.

The document name should come first. The secure-storage icon should follow the document name when relevant. The review rhythm should appear nearby in quiet italic text using simple cadence labels such as **monthly**, **quarterly**, **annually**, or **multiyear**.

### Rationale

This keeps the printable compact and makes the timing guidance easier to use at the point where the reader is reviewing each document.

### Operating Implication

The printable does not need separate Review Rhythm cards. The checklist itself can carry both storage guidance and review rhythm through concise visual markers.

## Product Format Rule: Digital-First, Not Traditional Printable Binder

### Decision

The first paid product should not be treated as a traditional printable binder. The word **Binder** may remain in the product name for clarity and search familiarity, but the actual product should be developed as a **digital-first household document system**.

### Rationale

Kay does not want to build an antiquated paper-binder product. The underlying need is modern household document control: what exists, where it lives, how it is organized, who can access it, what must be reviewed, and which physical originals need protected storage.

### Operating Implication

Future product work should start from digital organization: folder structure, naming conventions, digital inventory, review rhythm, access locator, and guidance for protected physical originals. Printable reference pages may be included only as supporting materials, not as the primary product concept.
