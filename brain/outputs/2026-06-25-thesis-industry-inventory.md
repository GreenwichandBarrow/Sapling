## Investor Feedback Pattern

The investors do not agree on a single next niche. The useful pattern is how they recommend selecting one.

- [[entities/jeff-stevens|Jeff Stevens]]: move in concentric circles from what Kay already learned. Do not cold-start if an adjacent circle carries forward customer knowledge, relationship access, or diligence vocabulary.
- [[entities/andrew-freiman|Andrew Freiman]]: keep the search mostly thesis-led, with room for opportunistic learning. The rough ratio is 70% thesis / 30% opportunistic.
- [[entities/michael-horowitz|Mike Horowitz]]: look for services that are mission-critical to an operating process, not merely required. Avoid checkbox compliance unless the service materially reduces pain or downtime.
- [[entities/sara-rosenthal|Sara Rosenthal]]: in pest, the ICP can be the wedge. Luxury/discretion/high-service customers may be more differentiating than a pest subtype.
- [[entities/luka-salamunic|Luka Salamunic]]: authenticity and edge matter more than volume. Searchers in energy-related sectors appear to be getting traction.
- [[entities/guillermo-lavergne|Guillermo Lavergne]] / Ibrahim: test whether the thesis has organic growth, enough target density, and a credible acquisition path before more modeling.
- [[entities/melissa-rosenblatt|Melissa Rosenblatt]] / [[entities/bk-growth|BK Growth]]: successful searchers tend to run industry-focused, in-person, river-guide-heavy searches. Copycat industries rarely work; the best opportunities are very specific niches.
- [[entities/sam-hyde|Sam Hyde]] / [[entities/steuart-botchford|Steuart Botchford]]: use advisors for concrete pattern recognition around specific companies, not abstract brainstorming alone.

## Current Operating Screen

A niche should pass most of these before becoming a serious lane:

1. There is a clear customer pain Kay can understand better than a generic searcher.
2. The market has enough target density at or near platform scale.
3. The revenue is recurring, reoccurring, contracted, or predictably repeatable.
4. The service is operationally important, not merely a cheap compliance checkbox.
5. Owner access can be built through river guides, conferences, associations, or existing relationships.
6. The middle of the market has not already been picked clean by PE.
7. Kay can explain her right-to-win without overrelying on luxury brand biography.

## What This Says About Picking The Next Niche

The highest-signal next search lane is not a single named industry yet. It is a pattern:

- service business,
- mission-critical or pain-reducing,
- recurring or predictably reoccurring,
- sold into a customer ecosystem Kay can understand,
- sourced through in-person river guides,
- with enough target density before PE has emptied the middle.

The best next experiments are:

1. **Commercial-property service adjacency screen**: pest-adjacent, fire safety, specialty cleaning, trash/waste-area, amenity management. Kill quickly if the market looks like pest: tiny operators plus expensive scaled firms.
2. **MRO / specialty equipment service screen**: specialty steel machinery, aerospace/industrial equipment, energy/water services. Focus on installed base, periodic maintenance, emergency repair, and OEM lockout.
3. **Luxury/high-value customer infrastructure screen**: fragrance/beauty quality testing, amenity management, marine/yacht services. The customer insight matters, but the business must still look like a search acquisition.
4. **Energy/water/utilities services screen**: worth a focused map because Luka and BK both gave external market signals.

## Fast Kill Rules

Kill or park a niche quickly if:

- every scaled company is PE-backed or strategic-owned;
- the only independent companies are sub-$500K EBITDA with no add-on path;
- the service is a cheap compliance checkbox;
- revenue is one-off project work without a predictable installed base;
- the right-to-win is only "Kay knows luxury" rather than a concrete buyer, vendor, or owner access advantage;
- the model is retail, restaurants, pure B2C, capital-intensive manufacturing, lending, or primary real estate.
