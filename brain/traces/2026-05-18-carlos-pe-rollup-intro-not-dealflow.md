# PE roll-up intro is an exit/intel channel, not a deal-flow source while buying

## Trigger

While iterating Kay's follow-up email to [[entities/carlos-nieto-dca|Carlos Nieto]] (DCA), the Miami PE roll-up intros were added as the lead ask — the [[calls/2026-05-13-carlos-nieto-dca]] call note's AI Analysis explicitly tags them "higher-value asset than teasers" and the action item says "Take Miami-PE rollup contacts forward." Kay challenged it: *"what am I going to do with a PE introduction… get their scraps? do i want that when they are looking at the same size now?"*

## Decision

**REJECT** including the Miami PE roll-up intro as a deal-flow ask in the Carlos email. Drop it entirely from the email; park the PE-relationship idea as a deliberate, separate long-game play (exit channel + market intel), not a now-ask routed through a busy intermediary.

## Alternatives considered

1. **Keep it as the lead ask** (what the call note recommended) — rejected. The same call note records that PE has moved *downstream* into G&B's exact $2–5M EBITDA band and is bidding 30–40% premiums on blue-collar. An intro to active roll-up operators while Kay is buying yields adverse-selected scraps (deals they passed for a reason) from people who are now her price competition. Not proprietary flow.
2. **Keep it, reframed as a soft long-game line in the email** — rejected. Trying to extract exit/intel value over a short email to a high-up intermediary cheapens it and muddies a clean two-ask message.
3. **Drop from email, pursue PE relationship separately later** — chosen. The genuine value (a) exit channel: a consolidator rolling up the space is the natural strategic acquirer at G&B's Jewel stage, and (b) market intel: knowing who pays the premiums tells Kay which sub-segments to avoid overpaying in. Neither is a sourcing request and neither is time-sensitive.

## Reasoning

The call note's "highest-value asset" label was correct about the *asset* but wrong about the *use*. A PE roll-up relationship is high-value as a future-exit and intelligence relationship, and low-value (negative, even) as a deal-flow source during the buy phase, because the counterparty is a same-band price competitor. The decision separates the relationship's value by lifecycle stage rather than treating "intro offered" as uniformly good. This is charter-aligned: G&B's 4-stage Bridge/Engine/Community/Jewel model makes the consolidator a buyer, not a supplier.

## Why this trace matters

A future agent reading the 5/13 call note (or any "intro to PE/strategic offered" signal) will see "highest-value, take forward" and re-add the ask. Without this trace it will reintroduce a request that is competitively self-defeating. The reusable rule: **an intro to an active acquirer in your own buy-band is not deal flow — evaluate acquirer relationships by lifecycle stage (exit/intel vs sourcing), not by "intro offered = good."**

## Key insight

"Higher-value asset" ≠ "request it now." Score acquirer relationships by *when* they pay off (exit/intel, later) vs *what you need now* (proprietary owner flow). Same-band consolidators are competitors at buy, customers at exit.
