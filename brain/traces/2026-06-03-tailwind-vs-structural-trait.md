# Tailwind vs structural trait on investor slides

## Trigger
On the pest-management CIM deck, the tailwinds slide was drafted as "Pest Control Demand is Recession-Proof, Recurring, and Regulatory-Driven," with four pillars: recession-proof, regulatory, climate-season-extension, and luxury-hospitality/wealth-transfer. Kay pushed back: "is this demand really a tailwind?" and then "the only true tailwind you mention on the slide is regulation."

## Decision
- REJECT recession-proof, recurring, and code-mandated as "tailwinds" — they are structural durability traits (downside protection, revenue-model quality), not directional growth forces. They belong on the Exec Summary / Search Fund Economics slides.
- REJECT climate-season-extension (too modest/gradual; commercial contracts run year-round regardless) and wealth-transfer/luxury-hospitality (a positioning story for the premium segment, not an aggregate-demand driver) as load-bearing tailwinds.
- APPROVE **regulatory expansion as the one genuine tailwind**, then reframe the whole slide around it as Direct vs Indirect regulatory effects.

## Alternatives Considered
- **Keep all four pillars as "tailwinds."** Rejected: conflates business-quality characteristics with growth drivers; an investor reads it as padding and discounts the slide.
- **Stretch the causal chain** (e.g., "wealth transfer grows the luxury customer base → more pest demand"). Rejected: Kay called this out as stretched; aggregate pest demand is driven by code, biology, and density, not consumer wealth.

## Reasoning
A tailwind in investor framing is a directional macro force *pushing growth forward* — the reference insurance deck got this right with "Hard Assets Are Becoming Institutionalized Financial Assets." Defensive/structural attributes (recurring, recession-proof, sticky) describe why revenue is *durable*, not why it is *growing*, and they have their own slides. Mislabeling them as tailwinds weakens the thesis and signals padding to a sophisticated reader.

## Why This Trace Matters
A future agent building any G&B investor deck will be tempted to list every attractive attribute under "Tailwinds." This decision draws the line: only directional growth-accelerating forces qualify; durability traits move to the economics/exec-summary slides. Without this, deck drafts will repeatedly conflate the two and require the same correction.

## Key Insight
Tailwind = directional growth force (e.g., expanding regulation creating new mandated demand). NOT a tailwind: recession-proof, recurring, code-mandated (durability traits), or a segment-positioning story dressed up as demand growth. Codified in [[feedback_tailwind_vs_structural_trait]].
