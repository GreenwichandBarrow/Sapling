## Trigger

Drafting a direct-to-owner pitch for a PA pest-control business on the Rejigg marketplace. The first draft's financing sentence read: *"I have committed capital in place to move quickly and close cleanly."* Kay corrected it: *"committed capital is not 100% accurate — I have investors behind me that have the first right of refusal. What I want the owner to know is that we can create a bespoke structure."*

## Decision

**REJECT "committed capital in place"; APPROVE a flexibility-led line:** *"I have investors behind me who give me the flexibility to build a bespoke structure around what matters most to you, whether that's price, timing, your team, or how involved you stay after a sale."*

## Alternatives Considered

1. **"Committed capital in place"** — punchy and reassuring, but factually inaccurate: Kay's investors hold a first right of refusal, not pre-committed capital. Overclaiming financing to a seller is a credibility/diligence risk.
2. **Softer flexibility-only line** (drop the backer mention entirely: "I have the flexibility to build a bespoke structure...") — avoids any PE/backed-buyer vibe, but loses the signal that Kay can actually fund a deal.
3. **"Investors behind me + bespoke structure" (chosen)** — accurate (backing without overclaiming), and leads with the owner's goals (deal shaped around price/timing/team/post-sale involvement) rather than a rigid take-it-or-leave-it offer.

## Reasoning

Two forces converge on the chosen line:
- **Accuracy.** "Investors behind me" honestly describes ROFR-style backing; "committed capital" implies a closed fund/escrow that doesn't exist. Sellers and their advisors test financing claims — an overclaim that unravels in diligence costs trust.
- **Differentiation.** The bespoke-structure framing is the real edge a personal searcher has over a strategic or PE buyer with a fixed playbook. Leading with "shaped around what matters most to you" is owner-centric (consistent with the owner-outreach doctrine: lead with THEM), where "committed capital" is buyer-centric.

This is distinct from the same-session Paul Giannamore DM, where explicit "search fund / lead investor" language was fine — Paul is an industry **advisor, not a seller**. The financing-framing constraint applies to direct-to-owner copy specifically.

## Why This Trace Matters

A future agent drafting owner outreach would, by default, reach for a confident "committed capital" line — it sounds strong. Without this correction it would both overclaim Kay's financing and bury the bespoke-structure differentiator that actually wins owners. This recurs on every direct-to-owner draft.

## Key Insight

For direct-to-owner copy: never claim "committed capital." Say "investors behind me" and lead with the **bespoke structure built around the owner's goals** (price, timing, team, post-sale involvement).
