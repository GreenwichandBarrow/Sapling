## Trigger

Kay wanted to email [[entities/becky-wuest-creavin|Becky]] (Peapack) to (a) mention the 6/2 Matt coffee, (b) ask whether Becky has a contact at **NYBB** she'd introduce, and (c) ask if Becky would be at the XPX Long Island event Friday 6/5. A clean draft was ready to send.

Mid-draft, two facts converged: the email's third ask ("will you be there?") was answerable — **Kay already knew Becky was on the XPX attendee list** — and the NYBB ask is an *introduction* request.

## Decision

**DEFER the send. Hold the NYBB intro ask + the catch-up for in person at XPX (Fri 6/5).** Do not send the email at all (a coordinate-only "let's find each other" note was offered as optional and also declined).

## Alternatives Considered

1. **Send the full email now** (the default). Gets the NYBB ask moving immediately. Cost: spends a warm intro ask over email — lower hit-rate — and asks "will you be there?" when the answer is already known, which reads as not having checked.
2. **Send a trimmed coordinate-only note** ("looking forward to XPX, let's connect"). Low-cost insurance against missing each other in a crowded room. Cost: an extra email for something that resolves naturally in person.
3. **Send nothing; raise NYBB + catch up in person (chosen).** The intro ask lands face-to-face at higher hit-rate; nothing is spent prematurely; no redundant email.

## Reasoning

Introduction asks are relationship currency. They convert far better in person than over email, and an in-person ask also lets Kay read Becky's willingness in real time. The only thing an email buys here is *coordination* (finding each other at the event) — and that value is near-zero when both parties are small-event attendees who will cross paths anyway. With attendance already confirmed, the email had no unique job left to do: its informational ask was self-answering and its relationship ask was better delivered in person.

The general default — "draft requested → send draft" — is wrong when the recipient is confirmed at a near-term event the sender is also attending. In that window, hold relationship asks for the room.

## Why This Trace Matters

A future agent, handed "draft an email to Becky asking for the NYBB intro," would draft and queue it to send. This trace says: first check whether the contact is confirmed at an upcoming event Kay is attending. If so, the higher-value move is to **hold the intro ask for in person** and send at most a light coordinate note — or nothing.

## Key Insight

When a warm contact is confirmed at an event you'll both attend within days, don't spend an introduction ask over email. Hold it for the room; email at most to make sure you connect.
