# "Premium" is a single angle, not the deck-wide frame

## Trigger
The pest CIM deck was drafted with "Premium Pest Management" as the title and recurring frame across cover, Exec Summary, and most section headers (mirroring the one-pager title). Kay: "I want that to be a single slide — or single angle about growth. not to be across the entire deck."

## Decision
- REJECT "Premium Pest Management" as the deck-wide title/thesis label.
- APPROVE keeping "premium commercial" as **one positioning slide** (the "Why Premium Commercial Captures the Tailwind" angle) and stripping "premium" from cover, Exec Summary, and other section titles — replaced with plain "Pest Management."
- Natural descriptive uses survive (e.g., "luxury hospitality" as a customer segment).

## Alternatives Considered
- **Keep "Premium" throughout** to signal the white-glove differentiation. Rejected: it over-claims the thesis and reads as a single positioning angle inflated into the whole story; the industry-section deck should establish the *industry* opportunity first, with premium-commercial as one lane within it.

## Reasoning
The deck's job is to establish the industry and the opportunity for investors; "premium" is one go-to-market angle, not the macro case. Leading every slide with it narrows the thesis prematurely and conflates positioning with industry attractiveness — the same category discipline as the tailwind correction ([[2026-06-03-tailwind-vs-structural-trait]]).

## Why This Trace Matters
A future agent building from the pest one-pager (titled "Premium Pest Management") will default to carrying that label across the whole deck. This decision says: the deck thesis is the *industry*; "premium commercial" is a single slide. Same logic likely applies to other niche decks built off premium-titled one-pagers.

## Key Insight
Don't let a one-pager's positioning title become a deck's thesis frame. Establish the industry opportunity deck-wide; confine the positioning angle (premium / white-glove) to its own slide. Internal scorecard numbers go to the appendix, not the investor-facing exec summary.
