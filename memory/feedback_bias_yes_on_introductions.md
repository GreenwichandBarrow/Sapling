Treat **personal introductions** and **specific deal pitches** as two different decisions with different default rules.

**The rules:**

- **Personal introduction (new person, new network node):** **default-accept.** The introduction IS the asset. The introducing person's pipeline may not fit Kay's thesis, the timing may be bad, the industry may be wrong — accept the meeting anyway. Propose a later time if needed. Do NOT decline.
- **Specific deal pitch from a known source:** **default-evaluate** against fit gates. If it fails the gates, decline cleanly AND gift calibration on what TO send next time. This educates the source for future deal-flow + preserves the relationship.

**Why:** Surfaced 2026-05-20 in `/socrates` session. Kay raised a specific prior incident: an earlier session advised her to decline two startup introductions (push to future date) because startups were not her thesis. She sent that message. The contact never replied again. Relationship lost. Kay: *"I'm also a little hesitant to take your advice to not pursue something or not take a phone call or not meet an owner. I think my bias should be towards the introduction."* Anchored in her independent-sponsor neighbor's rule: *"never turn down a conversation because you never know what can come of it."*

The conflation in prior sessions ("uniform evaluate-and-decline" applied to intros AND deal pitches identically) was hiding two different decisions and cost a relationship. The rules diverged this session and need to stay separate.

**How to apply:**
- **In AI drafting skill (when built):** classification runs BEFORE drafting. Routes inbound by type — personal intro → default-accept; deal pitch → fit-gate-evaluate; warm-network check-in → cadence; advisor counsel → strategic-response. Different templates per route.
- **In pipeline-manager / email-intelligence flagging:** when surfacing inbound for Kay's response queue, label by type. Intros never appear as "consider declining"; only as "schedule it" or "propose later if timing is bad."
- **In conversational recommendations from Claude:** if you find yourself recommending "decline this intro because the pipeline does not fit" — stop. The intro is not the pipeline. Recommend "accept the meeting; propose a time that works."
- **For advisor relationships:** reclassify advisors whose pipeline rarely fits (e.g., Cell-side digital-focused) from "decline or pursue" to "always meet, always warm." They are network nodes, not deal sources.
- **For warm-intro-finder skill:** treat every introduction in Kay's pipeline as a default-yes; only flag for human decision if it triggers a hard-exclude (e.g., known bad actor).
