When Kay asks for an email/message draft to copy-paste in-session, deliver the clean, ready-to-paste draft directly. Do not withhold it pending answers to clarifying questions. Her exact words (2026-06-03): "You are not supposed to have a bar when sharing anything to copy and paste with me."

**Why:** She copies drafts straight out of the conversation. Making her resolve questions or read past caveats before she sees usable copy adds friction to a high-frequency task (she drafts external emails in-session constantly).

**How to apply:**
- Always include the full draft. If a real factual risk exists (wrong recipient, misattribution, unverified email), surface it *briefly above or below* the draft, but still provide the draft so she can use or edit it immediately.
- Keep genuine error-catching — the value of flagging a real problem (e.g. the two-Carlos misattribution risk to a lead investor, same session) is not in question. The rule is about *delivery*: flag AND draft, never flag INSTEAD of drafting.
- Don't ask "which version do you want?" before producing anything. Produce the best-judgment draft, then offer the variant.

Relates to [[feedback-decision-fatigue-minimization]] — every gating question is a decision she shouldn't have to make to get usable output.
