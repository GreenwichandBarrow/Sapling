For Kay specifically, **industry is the OUTPUT of network access**, not the INPUT to it. Reverses conventional search-fund thesis structure.

**The rule:** when sequencing thesis work for Kay, ask first "which female-led associations or networks can she plug into in NY?" and let the industries those networks sit in be the thesis output. Do NOT default to "pick a sector → research the sector → find targets in the sector → warm-intro to targets." That is a national-volume-search-fund playbook that does not match how Kay has ever actually won.

**Why:** Surfaced 2026-05-20 in `/socrates` session. After 15 months of top-down industry selection (software, insurance, pest cycled in and out on financial/sector grounds), Kay rediscovered her own pre-MBA job-search methodology that had landed Chanel: 3 buckets of 10 named companies + network mapping + coffee with as many people as possible. She had INTENDED to use it for the search and drifted into the conventional playbook. Sam at the mid-search summit independently validated the same shape this week: *"5 companies, do everything to get the call."*

The lock-in evidence is consistent: every industry that stuck for Kay (Chanel, pest with women's pest association, insurance via a specific woman) was entered through a person/network, not through research. Every industry that dropped (software — the negative control, art storage when male-dominated, insurance after a discouraging large-strategic call) had no relational anchor or had it removed. See [[user-kay-women-led-purpose-throughline]] for the full pattern.

**How to apply:**

- **target-discovery skill calls:** prefer network-mapping-first when generating new candidate firms. Apollo / list-builder pulls should be filtered by "does a female-led association or network exist in this industry that Kay can plug into in NY?" before generating the list.
- **CIO agent (thesis coherence checks):** evaluate Kay's thesis cohesion on the **network-access axis**, not the conventional sector-depth axis. Cross-industry warm-intro optionality is structurally honest given her access pattern, not opportunism.
- **niche-intelligence skill:** when scoring an industry, include "female-led-network availability" as a primary lock-in criterion, weighted ahead of "investor palatability" or "competitive density."
- **Investor narrative:** position regional concentration as "sourcing concentration in NY where my edge produces highest-conviction conversations" — sourcing-logic frame, not a geographic restriction.
- **Negative-pattern flag:** if a future agent finds itself generating 1000-target buy-box-screen lists for Kay, that is the drift. Pull back to 3 buckets of 10 each.
- **/plan operational shape:** plan should sequence (1) which female-led networks to activate, (2) which industries fall out of those networks, (3) custom-send + warm intro to specific named firms within. NOT (1) which industry to pick, (2) outsource volume outreach, (3) hope something converts.
