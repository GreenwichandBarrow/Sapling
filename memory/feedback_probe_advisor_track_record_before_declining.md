When a buy-side advisor pitches an engagement that violates the success-fee-only doctrine ([[feedback-buyside-advisor-success-fee-only]]), do NOT default to a clean decline. If the advisor makes a verifiable claim about their strength in a niche G&B cares about, first send a reply probing their **recent transactions in that niche and the multiples playing out in the relevant EBITDA band** — framed around "what the engagement could look like" (ties the ask to the live negotiation; never "where the market sits today").

**Why:** A buy-side advisor's sales motion is a free intel channel. They'll hand over real comps while trying to win the engagement. Declining first closes that channel. The probe also tests the claim — E&K / Joe Vanore claimed "we'd have a lot of success here" in pest, but a 12-month scan of 347 emails showed zero pest listings (only landscaping/B2C). The probe surfaces that gap before any commitment.

**How to apply:** (1) Confirm the offer breaks doctrine and CFO affordability is at best tight. (2) Scan their email history for actual deal flow in the target niche. (3) If their claim and track record conflict, draft a chat-only reply (Kay sends in-thread) asking for recent niche transactions + multiples in G&B's band ($1–3M EBITDA for pest). (4) Defer engage/decline until they answer: credible closings + multiples → continue on success-fee-only terms; vague or off-thesis pivot → clean pass. Intel is useful to the thesis regardless. Grounding case: E&K / Joe Vanore, 2026-05-29 (trace `2026-05-29-buyside-advisor-intel-probe`). Related: [[feedback-quasi-decline-via-investor-hesitation]].
