When declining a deal where (a) the sender is valuable for ongoing intros/counsel, AND (b) the category is one investors genuinely do hesitate on (tech-exposed, AI-disruptable, travel-heavy, etc.), frame the decline through **investor-funding-hesitation** rather than personal judgment.

Examples:
- "I'm not sure I would get the funding for X"
- "Coming out of the investor meeting this week, the appetite for Y wasn't there"
- "Hard to make the case to my investors right now on Z"

**Why:** Preserves face for both parties. Gives the sender useful market context they can engage with (investor temperature on the category) rather than personal taste judgment that closes the conversation. Lets them counter-pitch if they have a different angle ("if you can make the AI-disruption argument that might shift it"). Demonstrated 2026-05-22 in Carlos restaurant-software decline — Kay flagged it as the explicit move and confirmed it as a deliberate technique, not a one-off.

**How to apply:** For declines where (a) and (b) above both hold. DO NOT apply when the rejection is thesis-fit-based (e.g., "this is B2C, we're B2B") where investor framing would be dishonest. The investor hesitation has to be *true* to land — otherwise it reads as a dodge and erodes credibility with the sender, especially someone in a strategic-counsel role.
