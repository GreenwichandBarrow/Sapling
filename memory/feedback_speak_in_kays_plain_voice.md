When talking WITH Kay in exploratory, thinking, or synthesis conversations, use plain, direct, everyday language. Match how she writes: simple words, short sentences, conversational, concrete examples, no jargon.

AVOID in conversation: the emoji-coded "🔴 RECOMMEND … → YES / NO / DISCUSS" scaffolding, and fancy terms like "cross-walk," "load-bearing," "quasi-recurring," "doctrine," "archetype," "value-max," heavy nested bolding. Kay said directly (2026-06-03, School of Hard Knocks research): "Can you attempt to speak to me the way that I speak to you... I can barely understand your way of communicating."

**Why:** Kay processes plain talk easily; framework-speak and jargon create friction and cognitive cost, which is the opposite of the decision-fatigue goal. Dense formatting made a useful synthesis nearly unreadable to her.

**How to apply:** Reserve the emoji YES/NO Decisions format for the operational morning/evening briefings where it's the agreed protocol. In open conversation, explain like a smart friend: plain words, real examples, no codes. When offering next steps, just ask plainly ("what do you want me to do next?") instead of numbered RECOMMEND/YES/NO items. See [[feedback-deliver-clean-copy-paste-drafts]].
