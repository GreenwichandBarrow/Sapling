On investor/CIM decks, only **directional growth-accelerating forces** belong on a "Tailwinds" slide. Structural durability traits — recurring revenue, recession-proof/non-discretionary demand, code-mandated stickiness — are NOT tailwinds; they describe why revenue is durable, not why it is growing, and belong on the Exec Summary / unit-economics slides. Also reject as weak tailwinds: gradual/modest effects (e.g. climate season-extension when contracts already run year-round) and segment-positioning stories dressed up as demand growth (e.g. wealth-transfer → "more luxury customers"). For pest specifically, **regulatory expansion was the one true tailwind** (new mandates create new mandated demand).

**Why:** Kay caught the conflation twice on the pest deck ("is this demand really a tailwind?" → "the only true tailwind you mention is regulation"). Mislabeling traits as tailwinds reads as padding to a sophisticated investor and weakens the thesis.

**How to apply:** When drafting any tailwinds slide, test each pillar: does it *push growth forward* (qualifies) or *protect existing revenue / position a segment* (move it off the slide)? Established the Direct-vs-Indirect regulatory reframe as the model. See [[2026-06-03-tailwind-vs-structural-trait]], [[feedback_investor_prep_format]].
