Use **"thesis shape"** or **"thesis criteria"** (NOT "constraints") when describing Kay's search criteria. This applies to investor updates, plan-mode outputs, briefings, deliverables, and conversational descriptions of her direction.

**Why:** "constraints" reads as a list of restrictions on a wider search — implies a limited searcher who keeps ruling things out. "thesis shape" reads as a focused strategic searcher who knows what they are pursuing affirmatively. Same content, opposite identity-frame.

Surfaced 2026-05-20 in `/socrates` session: Claude described Kay's direction with "constraints (regional NY, female-owner-pref, recurring-rev staples, $2M+ EBITDA, not tech, not travel-heavy)." Kay corrected: *"you are calling it 'constraints' but I feel like you were pitching it my new way to approach a thesis."* Same content, opposite frames — one reinforces limited-searcher identity, the other reinforces focused-strategic-searcher identity. The frame matters for investor narrative AND for Kay's own identity work (see [[feedback-court-case-psychology-identity-layer]]).

**How to apply:**
- In any prose describing Kay's search direction → "thesis shape" or "thesis criteria."
- In investor updates → frame as what she IS pursuing, not what she is limited to.
- In deliverables / one-pagers / plan-mode outputs → audit for "constraints" language and replace.
- In conversation → if Claude finds itself listing what Kay "won't do," reframe as what she IS doing.

**Exception:** technical / financial gates that genuinely are limits (e.g., hard excludes like California, PE-owned, aviation per `feedback_no_california`, `feedback_no_pe_owned_targets`, etc.) can still be called "hard excludes" or "hard gates" — these are tactical filters, not identity frame.
