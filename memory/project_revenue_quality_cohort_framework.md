## The revenue-quality 5-tier spectrum (best → worst)
1. **Contractual recurring** — auto-renewing contracts. CAC-immune (you don't re-acquire the customer).
2. **Non-contractual recurring** — habitual repurchase, no contract.
3. **Repeat** — comes back but not on a cycle.
4. **Actuarial** — predictable in aggregate, not per-customer.
5. **Transactional** — one-off. Most CAC-exposed; a melting asset if churn is high.

**Contract-penalty caveat:** "a 3-year contract with a 30-day out is really a 30-day contract." Score by the cancellation term, not the stated length.

## Retention floors and cohort doctrine
- Floor: **94-96% gross retention, 100%+ net** (expansion offsets churn).
- **Cohort analysis is the default DD expectation.** If the seller doesn't track it, you build it from raw data during diligence. A flat, high-retention cohort that keeps spending years out = sticky / referral-fed; a decaying cohort = treadmill buying replacement customers at rising cost. Same revenue line, opposite businesses — invisible without the cohort cut.

## Market-cooling data (Market Update session)
- Acquisition **close rates fell from ~54% (2022 cohort) to the 30s (2023 cohort)** despite high LOI volume. The bar to close is higher than ever; it's a **"find creativity in deal structure"** market.

## How this resolves the AI-CAC / feeder-demand question
The 5/31 socrates thread started from Kay's observation that **client acquisition cost is ~10x higher in sales-driven services** (commercial cleaning, pest) because AI is collapsing the sales/marketing edge. The reframe: rising CAC doesn't just haircut growth, it **moves the risk from the growth line to the retention line** (harder to see in a CIM) and makes the existing book the whole value.

The tier spectrum is the instrument: **CAC-exposure only bites the bottom three tiers.** So you do NOT screen by industry label — you screen by where a *specific business* sits on the spectrum. Pest isn't one tier: auto-renewing quarterly contracts + route density = tier 1-2 (CAC-immune); one-off call-out work = tier 4 (melting). Same NAICS code, opposite businesses.

**First-call proxy screen** (before cohort data shows up in DD): *"What % of revenue is under contract, and what's the cancellation term?"* — cheap to ask, separates feeder/channeled demand from the CAC battle.
