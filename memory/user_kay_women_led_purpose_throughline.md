Kay's organizing principle across her entire post-MBA professional life is **supporting women in business** — women-led businesses, women-owned acquisition targets, women-led networks, and a post-search vision of returning to the search community as a female investor. This is not a search-fund-era preference. It runs from her career choices through philanthropy through search interests through stated post-exit goals.

**SHARPENED 2026-05-21 (the load-bearing rule — read this first):** the principle is **women-led businesses IN MALE-DOMINATED industries.** Not "women-led" alone. Two conditions, both required: (1) the **leader must be a woman**; (2) the **surrounding industry environment must be predominantly male** (workforce, leadership composition, customers, vendors, field ops — by any of these). **Female-dominated industries are EXCLUDED even when the leader is a woman.** This is generative and chosen, not reactive: Kay has *actively preferred* male-dominated environments her whole life (science competitions, enrichment programs chosen because they were mainly male; fashion chosen for its male strategy floor though the working population was female; Chanel — stayed for the male CEO/real-estate work, struggled with the female day-to-day). She explicitly does **not** want to work surrounded by women day-to-day — which is *why she walked away from women's health.* Test cases: aerospace w/ female leader **PASSES**; art storage **PASSES** (she likes it — male-dominated field is the draw); pest **PASSES**; women-led marketing agency **FAILS** (female-dominated); women's health / Myself Renewed **FAILS** retroactively. Full reasoning: `brain/outputs/2026-05-21-discussion-women-led-male-environment-thesis.md`. Industries are still selected up front, but by a *different rule* (male-dominated env + female-led network + NY access + travel-light) — not skipped. Thesis is downstream of this principle; prior thesis-spin came from compressing the principle INTO the thesis.
